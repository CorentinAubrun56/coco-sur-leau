In the Mini class, not all races are equal! They are ranked by **level**, according to their length. And that level matters a lot, because it decides what I'm allowed to race… and what gets me closer to the Mini Transat.

## The 4 levels

| Level | What it means | Examples |
| :---: | --- | --- |
| **D** | One-day event | |
| **C** | No race longer than 300 miles | Plastimo Lorient Mini, La Pornichet Select, Trophée Marie-Agnès Péron |
| **B** | At least one race longer than 300 miles | Mini en Mai, Mini-Fastnet, Puru Transgascogne |
| **A** | At least one race longer than 1,000 miles | The Mini Transat |

For reference, 1 nautical mile = 1.852 km. A 300-mile race is already 2 to 3 days at sea, alone or two-handed on 6.5 metres!

## You can't just race anywhere

- **To race a level C event**, you must have finished a level C or B race in the last 5 years, **or** sailed 24 hours non-stop on your boat (with a logbook to prove it).
- **To race a level B event**, you must have finished a level C or B race in the last 5 years, **or** completed the qualifying course outside races (see below).

So you start with level C races, then move up to level B. No shortcuts!

## What it takes for the Mini Transat

Two big steps, both **on the boat I'll race the Transat with**, so Big Bounce:

### 1. 1,500 race miles

At least **1,500 miles** in races on the Mini calendar, with:

- at least **2 races**, including at least **one solo**;
- at least one race of **more than 500 miles non-stop**;
- and in the year of the Transat, at least **one level C or B race** finished.

Careful: only **finished** races count, and the miles credited are those in the notice of race (not what the log says after a lot of tacking!).

### 2. The qualifying course: 1,000 miles solo

A **1,000-mile loop, solo, non-stop and unassisted**, outside any race. In the Atlantic, you have to round three marks: the **Coningbeg** buoy south of Ireland, the **Rochebonne plateau** and the **Île de Ré**. Handwritten logbook, sextant fixes, photos at every mark… it's a real rite of passage, about a week at sea.

## Why speed matters

Places on the start line are limited. They are given out **in chronological order**, counting from the date you've completed everything (entry, race miles, qualifying course). The sooner I finish, the better my chances. Follow my tally on the [Ranking]({{ '/en/ranking/' | relative_url }}) page.

## What about "Proto" and "Series"?

That's the category of the **boat**, not the race. **Prototypes** are one-off boats with a lot of design freedom. **Series** boats are production boats, all identical. Both race together, but with separate rankings.

**Big Bounce, number 1019, is a proto!** So I race in the Prototype category.
